How Online Stores Manufacture Fake "-45% OFF" Discounts

Before Black Friday, Prime Day, Cyber Week, or seasonal clearances, red percentage badges flood every product page. Yet independent price tracking consistently shows that many headline discounts deliver zero savings over normal everyday street pricing.

Tactic 1: Stale Launch MSRP / UVP Anchoring

A pair of headphones launches at €399 in year one, settles around €229 at almost every store in year two, and gets tagged as "UVP €399 — Save 42%" during promotional weeks at €229. Because RealPriceNoLies compares against the rolling 90-day market average instead of the launch MSRP, that fake 42% discount scores as ordinary standard retail.

Tactic 2: The Pre-Promo Price Hike

Even under the EU Price Indication Directive (Omnibus Directive, requiring prior 30-day lowest price disclosure), retailers sometimes raise a SKU to full retail for several weeks prior to a major sales campaign so the subsequent "sale drop" meets the technical 30-day rule while exceeding what the item cost 60 or 90 days ago. Looking at a 180-day curve catches this immediately.

Tactic 3: Marketplace & Variant Baiting

Clicking an advertised color or capacity often swaps you to a slower third-party seller listing above the store average—or hides lower prices available simultaneously from competing EU retailers like MediaMarkt, Saturn, Galaxus, Coolblue, Otto, or Zalando.

Your 10-Second Pre-Checkout Checklist

  • Check the 90-day average and 180-day low rather than the crossed-out red price.
  • Strip affiliate/tracking query clutter so you share and inspect clean canonical URLs.
  • Compare at least 3–5 competing stores factoring in shipping costs before tapping Buy Now.
